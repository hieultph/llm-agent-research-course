## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): the study design, the handling of data, the reporting of statistics and the materials needed to reproduce the work must reach the ordinary peer-review standard of the field. From a cross-disciplinary standpoint this matters because a reader from clinical practice or anthropology must be able to trust that the reported effects come from the design and not from artefacts of construction; I do not score this dimension.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat): claims must match current evidence in the domain, prior work must be represented correctly, and domain terminology and results must be free of factual error. Adjacent-field readers depend on this, since misused clinical or cultural terms propagate quickly into applied settings; I do not score this dimension.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate with the methodology seat also eligible): the central thesis must hang together, the evidence must actually support what is claimed, and no reasoning fallacy may undermine the core argument. For an outside reader this means the conclusions drawn for practice should follow from what was measured; I do not score this dimension.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, owned by my perspective seat): the framing, definitions and stated implications must be understandable to readers in neighbouring fields, and any claim that crosses disciplinary lines must be substantiated. For me this means that clinical constructs (urgency levels, red-flag presentations, reference standards) and cultural constructs (lay illness categories, home remedies) are defined in ways a clinician or medical anthropologist would accept, that cultural material is represented faithfully rather than as caricature, and that implications for deployed health tools and for any released data are stated with appropriate safety framing.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief seat): organisation, clarity, figure and table quality and conformance with venue conventions. Clear exposition is what lets an adjacent-field reader find definitions and limitations; I do not score this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief seat): the manuscript must suit the configured venue and offer an original, significant contribution for that readership. Whether interdisciplinary value counts toward significance is a venue judgement I leave to that seat; I do not score this dimension.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether clinical labels and the reference standard are defined in terms an emergency or primary-care clinician would recognise and validate for the target population; whether lay illness concepts and home remedies are described accurately, with sourcing, without exoticising or stereotyping, and with acknowledgement of their legitimate cultural meaning; whether implications for consumer health apps, under-triage harm, and risks of any released data are stated concretely and proportionately for adjacent-field readers.
what_triggers_block: Interdisciplinary claims that drive the paper's practical or safety conclusions rest on clinical labels or cultural characterisations with no stated clinical validation or source, or the representation of a lay illness concept is materially misleading or stereotyping, or released material could plausibly be used as clinical guidance without any harm or misuse safeguard.
what_triggers_warn: Clinical or cultural constructs are usable but under-defined or under-sourced for adjacent readers, the limits of label validity without qualified clinical review are acknowledged only briefly, or practical implications for health tools and data release are stated vaguely or overgeneralised beyond the studied population.
